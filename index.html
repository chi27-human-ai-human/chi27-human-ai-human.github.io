From Human-AI to Human-AI-Human

Designing Generative AI for Users and the People Affected by Their Use

Pittsburgh, PA, USA | May 10–14, 2027 | One-Day, In Person

About the Workshop

Generative AI (GenAI) increasingly shapes decisions, actions, and outputs that affect people beyond those who directly interact with it. Yet GenAI use that serves a direct user’s goals may not account for the needs, contexts, and relationships of those who ultimately experience its consequences, negatively affecting their experiences, well-being, and relationships.

Moving beyond a focus on direct Human–AI interaction, this workshop advances a Human–AI–Human perspective, asking:

How should GenAI be designed and developed to better account for other people and relationships affected by users’ AI-assisted decisions, actions, and outputs?

Addressing this question opens a broader research and design space spanning GenAI interactions and interventions, affected others’ participation in shaping and contesting GenAI use, and support beyond the interface.

Expected Outcomes

Bringing together researchers and practitioners from academia, industry, and nonprofits, we aim to develop two outcomes:

01

A Cross-Domain Collection of Cases & Challenges

Concrete cases documenting diverse settings in which direct users’ GenAI use affects others, including the impacts, challenges, and open research questions these cases raise.

02

Future Research & Design Directions

Directions for GenAI systems, interactions, and interventions that help users account for affected others and enable affected others to shape or contest GenAI use, as well as broader support when these challenges cannot reasonably be addressed through the interface or by direct users alone.

Following the workshop, we will develop the case collection into a publicly available Human–AI–Human case library and synthesize the research and design directions into a workshop report. More broadly, we aim to build a cross-sector community around this emerging research space.

Call for Participation

GenAI increasingly enables users to generate and act in ways that affect others. This workshop explores research and design opportunities from a Human–AI–Human perspective, focusing on how GenAI could help users account for the people and relationships affected by their AI-assisted decisions, actions, and outputs. Through group discussions and design activities, participants will map cases and challenges, identify research directions for AI interactions that account for affected others during GenAI use, examine key tensions and open questions, and explore support beyond the interface.

We welcome researchers, designers, practitioners, and community partners interested in this emerging space.

Submission Requirements: Please submit one of the following through Google Form:

Position Paper

1–3 pages

Present your motivation and a relevant case and its challenges, design concept, or research approach to an open question using the ACM Master Article Format.

Statement of Interest

1–2 pages

Describe your motivation, relevant research, practice, community, or lived experience, and expected contribution.

Concurrent Submission

Work under review or published

Submit relevant work under review, accepted, or recently published elsewhere, with a brief statement of its relevance and your motivation for participating.

Submissions will be selected based on relevance and potential contribution while seeking diversity in backgrounds, domains, methods, and perspectives. We expect approximately 30 participants. At least one author of each accepted submission must attend the workshop. At least two workshop organizers will be present to facilitate the workshop. The workshop will also be open to CHI attendees, subject to capacity. With authors' permission, accepted position papers will be published on the workshop website and archived through CEUR-WS or arXiv.

Workshop Activities

Session 1 | 80 min

Introduction & Framing | 30 min

Welcome, participant introductions, and a brief overview of the workshop goals, emerging research, and activities.

Activity 1: Mapping the Problem Space | 50 min

In small groups, participants will examine cases where GenAI use affects others, mapping the direct user, affected others, their relationships, and the AI-assisted decisions, actions, or outputs involved. Groups will identify key impacts and challenges, surface empirical questions and methods, and compare cases to identify recurring and context-specific challenges. The activity will conclude with a brief lightning share.

Break | 20 min

Informal networking and continued conversation.

Session 2 | 90 min

Activity 2: Exploring Interaction Concepts and Their Implications | 40 min

Building on the cases and questions identified in Activity 1, each group will select a prompt and explore AI interactions and interventions that help direct users account for affected others, enable affected others to shape or contest GenAI use, or mediate among multiple parties. Groups will sketch an interaction concept, consider the tensions and tradeoffs it may raise for direct users, affected others, and their relationships, and discuss how its effects could be studied and evaluated. The activity will conclude with a brief lightning share.

Activity 3: Looking Beyond the Interface | 40 min

Groups will examine where interaction-level approaches may be insufficient for accounting for affected others and their relationships, and explore broader forms of support beyond individual users and interaction design. These may include organizational and community support, as well as AI system and model development. The activity will conclude with a brief lightning share of key directions.

Closing | 10 min

We will revisit insights across the three activities, discuss next steps for documenting and sharing workshop outcomes, and identify opportunities for continued collaboration.
