From Human-AI to Human-AI-Human

Designing AI for Users and the People Affected by Their Use

Pittsburgh, PA, USA | May 10–14, 2027 | One-Day, In Person

About the Workshop

Generative AI (GenAI) increasingly shapes decisions, actions, and outputs that affect people beyond those who directly interact with it. Yet AI that serves a direct user’s goals may not account for the needs, contexts, and relationships of those who ultimately experience its consequences, potentially affecting their experiences, well-being, and relationships.

Moving beyond a focus on direct Human–AI interaction, this workshop advances a Human–AI–Human perspective, asking how AI can support users in accounting for the people affected by their GenAI use. Bringing together researchers and practitioners from academia, industry, and nonprofits, we aim to develop (1) a cross-domain collection of cases and challenges documenting how direct users’ GenAI use affects others and the open questions these cases raise, and (2) research and design directions for developing and evaluating AI interactions, interventions, and broader organizational and community support. More broadly, we aim to build a community interested in advancing this research space.

Call for Participation

GenAI increasingly shapes decisions, actions, and outputs that affect not only direct users but also others who experience the consequences of its use. This CHI workshop explores new research and design opportunities from a Human–AI–Human perspective, considering the people and relationships affected by GenAI use. Through small-group discussions and collaborative design activities, participants will map cases and challenges across domains and identify future directions for AI interactions and interventions, as well as broader organizational and community support. We welcome researchers, designers, practitioners, and community partners from academia, industry, nonprofits, and other sectors interested in advancing this emerging research space.

Submission Requirements: Please submit one of the following through our workshop website:

  • Position Paper (1–3 pages): Using the ACM Master Article Format, present a relevant case, interaction or intervention, research approach, key tension, or open question.
  • Statement of Interest (1–2 pages): Describe your motivation, relevant research, practice, community, or lived experience, and expected contribution to the workshop.
  • Concurrent Submission: Submit relevant work currently under review, accepted, or recently published elsewhere, with a brief statement describing its relevance to the workshop and your motivation for participating.

Submissions will be selected based on relevance and potential contribution while seeking diversity across backgrounds, domains, methods, and perspectives. We expect approximately 30 participants. At least one author of each accepted submission and one workshop organizer must attend. With authors’ permission, accepted position papers will be published on the workshop website and in CEUR-WS proceedings.

Workshop Activities

Session 1 | 75 min

Introduction & Framing | 30 min

Welcome, participant introductions, and a brief overview of the workshop goals, key concepts, and activities.

Activity 1: Mapping the Problem Space | 45 min

In small groups, participants will use a shared mapping template to unpack cases where GenAI use affects others, mapping direct users, affected others, their relationships, potential impacts and challenges, and open research questions. Groups will compare their cases to identify recurring and context-specific challenges.

Break | 20 min

Informal networking and continued conversation.

Session 2 | 90 min

Activity 2: Exploring Interaction & Research Opportunities | 40 min

Building on their case maps, groups will sketch interaction concepts and interventions that could help direct users account for affected others, enable affected others to participate or contest AI use, or mediate among multiple parties. Groups will then discuss how these concepts could be evaluated and identify key tensions and open research questions.

Activity 3: Looking Beyond the Interface | 35 min

Groups will revisit cases where their interaction concepts may not be enough and identify why, such as limits in representation, authority, or opportunities for contestation. They will then map organizational and community structures that could address these gaps, including participation processes, feedback channels, shared practices, and mechanisms for distributing responsibility.

Closing & Synthesis | 15 min

Groups will share key takeaways, and we will synthesize emerging research and design directions and discuss opportunities for continued collaboration.
